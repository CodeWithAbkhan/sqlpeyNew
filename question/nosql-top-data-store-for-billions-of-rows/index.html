What is the best data store for billions of rows?

When it comes to storing a large amount of data, especially billions of rows, choosing the right data store is crucial. The requirements for this use case are fast inserts and lookups for all records with the same GUID and the ability to access the data store from .net.

There are many data stores available, including traditional relational databases and newer NoSQL databases. Each option has its pros and cons, and the choice depends on the specific needs and requirements of the use case.

Why choose NoSQL?

The OP mentions considering NoSQL databases due to their optimizations for distributed queries and scaling. NoSQL databases are designed to handle large amounts of unstructured or semi-structured data, which can make them a good fit for this use case.

Although, foremost to note that NoSQL databases can come with trade-offs, such as reduced consistency and reliability compared to traditional relational databases. Therefore, it’s important to consider the specific requirements for the use case before choosing a NoSQL database.

What are the availability, reliability, recoverability, and consistency requirements?

Before deciding on a specific data store, it’s essential to consider the availability, reliability, recoverability, and consistency requirements.

  • Availability: What availability requirement do you have? Is 99.999% uptime necessary, or is 95% enough?
  • Reliability: What reliability requirement do you have? Does missing an insert cost you $1M?
  • Recoverability: What recoverability requirement do you have? If you lose one day of data, does it matter?
  • Consistency: What consistency requirement do you have? Does a write need to be guaranteed to be visible on the next read?

If all these requirements are essential, then storing 36 billion small, flat records will cost millions in hardware and licensing on a relational system, no matter what gimmicks are tried (sharding, partitioning, etc.). A NoSQL system will, by definition, not meet all these requirements.

What NoSQL database should I use?

After reviewing the requirements and knowing the trade-offs, it’s time to choose the right NoSQL database.

There are many NoSQL databases to choose from, including Cassandra, MongoDB, HBase, and more. Each option has different strengths, weaknesses, and use cases. It’s essential to evaluate each option’s features and align them with the specific requirements of the use case.

How should I store the data in a NoSQL database?

When storing billions of records, it’s important to consider the data’s structure and the database’s performance. Here is a suggestion for storing the data:

  • Key Structure: Use GUID for the primary key in the NoSQL database.
  • Partitioning: Partition the data so that records with the same GUID are stored in the same partition. This will optimize the queries for the specific GUID.
  • Time-Based Clustering: Cluster the table by time, so records with the same GUID and timestamp are stored together. This will optimize lookups for recent records.

Storing 36 billion small, flat records is no easy feat, but with careful planning and the right data store, it can be accomplished. Consider the specific requirements of the use case, evaluate the different data stores, and choose the option that aligns best with the needs of the project. ]]>
