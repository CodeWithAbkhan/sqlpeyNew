NOSQL: Apache Cassandra’s Approach to Aggregate Operations How does Apache Cassandra handle aggregate operations?
If you are new to Apache Cassandra and NoSQL in general, you may be wondering how the database handles aggregate operations such as SUM() and COUNT(). In this Q&A Session, we will explore how Cassandra does aggregate operations and what alternatives are available.

What are aggregate operations?

In SQL, aggregate operations refer to statistical calculations performed on groups of rows or columns in a table. These calculations include functions like SUM(), COUNT(), AVERAGE(), MIN(), MAX(), and GROUP BY. They are typically used to answer questions like “What is the total revenue in my sales table?” or “How many customers do I have in each state?”.

Does Apache Cassandra support aggregate operations?

Although Apache Cassandra is a amazing NoSQL database, it is not designed for complex calculations and data analysis. Instead, Cassandra focuses on fast data writes and lookups. It is not possible to perform aggregate operations like SUM(), AVERAGE(), or GROUP BY in Cassandra.

How can I perform aggregate operations in Cassandra?

As mentioned, Cassandra does not support aggregate operations out of the box. Although, there are a few alternatives you can consider:

1. Pre-Compute Aggregates

One approach is to pre-compute your aggregates before inserting your data into Cassandra. For example, if you want to calculate the total revenue of your sales table, you can do this calculation outside of Cassandra and insert the result as a new column in your table. This approach is useful if you have a small number of aggregates and they don’t change frequently.

2. Client-Side Aggregation

Another option is to perform aggregate calculations client-side. In this approach, you retrieve all the data you need from Cassandra and then perform the calculations using a client-side programming language like Python or Java. This approach is useful if you have a small amount of data or if your aggregates are simple.

3. Use an Analytics Tool

If you have a large amount of data or your aggregates are complex, you may need to use an analytics tool like Apache Spark or Apache Flink. These tools allow you to perform distributed data processing and analytics across large clusters and provide more advanced statistical functions.

Recap

Apache Cassandra is not designed for complex calculations and aggregate operations. While it does provide fast data write and lookup speeds, it lacks advanced statistical functions like SUM(), AVERAGE(), and GROUP BY found in SQL.
